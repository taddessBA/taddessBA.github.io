Beza A. Taddess

PhD Candidate in Sociology, Princeton University

I am a PhD candidate in Sociology at Princeton University. I study the biology of social inequality — how conditions like poverty, racism, and caregiving leave measurable traces in the body, and what those traces predict about the lives people go on to lead.

Most of my work follows families. I combine genetic and longitudinal data — epigenetic clocks, polygenic indices, panel studies spanning decades — with designs that can carry causal weight, including sibling comparisons and natural experiments. Recent papers ask when in early childhood family income matters most for a child’s biological aging, whether having children accelerates a parent’s, and what a self-rated health score actually measures.
